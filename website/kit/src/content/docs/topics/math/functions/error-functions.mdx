<Intuition>
Picture the bell curve. Pick a distance either side of the middle and shade
everything between. The **error function** tells you what fraction of the total
area you just shaded: near zero for a narrow band, approaching 1 as the band
widens to swallow the whole curve.
</Intuition>

**Where you meet it.** "Three sigma" is a claim about the error function. So is
every p-value from a normal test, every process-capability index on a factory
floor, and the confidence interval on any measurement assumed to be normally
distributed. It also turns up far from statistics — heat diffusion and signal
attenuation are described by the same integral.

Formally, <Api pkg="math-analysis" name="erf" /> computes
`erf(x) = 2/√π ∫₀ˣ e^(−t²) dt`, which is the probability that a standard normal
measurement lands within `x√2` of its mean.

## The complement is not a subtraction

<Api pkg="math-analysis" name="erfc" /> computes `1 − erf(x)` — the area
*outside* the band rather than inside it. That is the quantity you want for a
tail probability: "how unlikely is a result at least this extreme?"

It exists as its own function because computing it as a subtraction destroys it.
`erf(x)` reaches exactly `1` in floating point at about `x = 6`, and `1 - 1` is
`0`. Everything past that point — every tail probability smaller than about
`1e-17` — is gone.

Computed directly, `erfc` keeps full relative precision all the way down to the
underflow limit near `x = 26.5`, which is what makes a tail probability of
`1e-100` a number rather than a zero.

<Example id="math-analysis/erf-erfc" />

The rule generalises across this whole package:

> **Reach for the upper form when the answer is small.**

The same pairing appears in
[the incomplete gamma family](/node-essentials/topics/math/functions/incomplete-gamma-beta/),
for exactly the same reason.
