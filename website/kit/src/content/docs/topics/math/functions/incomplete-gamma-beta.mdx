<Intuition>
[The gamma and beta functions](/node-essentials/topics/math/functions/gamma/)
each describe the area under a whole curve. Stop partway along and you get an
**incomplete** version: the area up to that point. Divide by the whole and you
get a fraction between 0 and 1 — which is exactly the question "what proportion
of outcomes are no bigger than this?"

That fraction is a **cumulative distribution function**, and these four are the
ones that statistics runs on.
</Intuition>

**Where you meet them.** Every time software reports a p-value it evaluates one
of these. A chi-squared test goes through the gamma family; a *t*-test, an
*F*-test and a binomial confidence interval all go through the beta family. If
you have ever read `p = 0.003` off a screen, this is the code that produced it.

**Regularized** means the normalized version, in `[0, 1]`. The word is in the
name because libraries disagree about what the bare name means, and the
difference is a factor of `Γ(a)` that no type system will catch for you.

## The gamma family

<Api pkg="math-analysis" name="regularizedIncompleteGamma" /> computes `P(a, x)`
— the proportion of a `Gamma(a, 1)` distribution at or below `x`. It is the
cumulative function of the gamma, chi-squared and Poisson distributions.

<Api pkg="math-analysis" name="regularizedIncompleteGammaUpper" /> computes
`Q(a, x) = 1 − P(a, x)`, the proportion *above* `x`. In practice this is the
p-value, and it is a separate function for the same reason
[`erfc` is](/node-essentials/topics/math/functions/error-functions/): when the
answer is small, subtracting it out of 1 has already thrown it away.

## The beta family

<Api pkg="math-analysis" name="regularizedIncompleteBeta" /> computes
`Iₓ(a, b)`, the cumulative function of the Beta distribution — the distribution
of a *proportion*. Through it come Student's *t*, the *F* distribution and the
binomial tail, which is why it appears far more often than its name suggests.

## Running them backwards

A cumulative function answers "which percentile is this value at?". Very often
you want the other direction: "which value sits at the 95th percentile?" That is
the **quantile**, and it is what turns a confidence level into a critical value.

<Api pkg="math-analysis" name="inverseRegularizedIncompleteGamma" /> and
<Api pkg="math-analysis" name="inverseRegularizedIncompleteBeta" /> do that
inversion. Neither has a closed form, so both refine an estimate until it stops
moving — see
[convergence and iteration limits](/node-essentials/topics/math/calculus/convergence/)
for how that termination is controlled.

An inverse is only as good as the round trip it survives: put a probability in,
get a value out, feed that value back through the forward function, and see how
far you have drifted.
