<Intuition>
A decimal expansion builds a number by adding ever-smaller pieces:
`3 + 0.1 + 0.04 + …`. A **continued fraction** builds it by nesting ever-deeper
divisions instead:

```text
b₀ + a₁ / (b₁ + a₂ / (b₂ + a₃ / (b₃ + ⋯)))
```

It goes on forever, but the deeper terms matter less and less, so stopping
somewhere gives an answer — and for many functions it closes in on the true
value far faster than the equivalent sum would.
</Intuition>

**Where you meet it.** Mostly underneath other things. The
[incomplete gamma and beta functions](/node-essentials/topics/math/functions/incomplete-gamma-beta/)
in this package are computed with continued fractions, because their power
series becomes unusably slow exactly where the fraction becomes fast. Outside
numerics they are how "best rational approximation" problems are solved — the
reason a year is 365 + 97/400 days in the Gregorian calendar.

## Evaluating one

<Api pkg="math-analysis" name="continuedFractionEval" /> takes a
<Api pkg="math-analysis" name="ContinuedFraction" />: two functions giving the
`aⱼ` and `bⱼ` coefficients for any depth. Supplying them as functions rather than
arrays is what lets a genuinely unbounded fraction be described in a few lines.

The evaluation works *forwards*, from `b₀` outward, so it never has to decide in
advance how deep to go. Each step multiplies the running value by a factor that
tends towards 1, and the iteration stops once that factor is within tolerance of
1 — in other words, once another term would no longer change the answer.

The alternative, working backwards from the deepest term, has to pick a depth
first and start over when the pick turns out to have been too shallow.

<Example id="math-analysis/continued-fraction-eval" />

Like every iterative routine here, it can run out of budget before it runs out
of work. It reports that rather than throwing — see
[convergence and iteration limits](/node-essentials/topics/math/calculus/convergence/).
